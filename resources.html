The basics

FSA and HSA, in plain words.

An FSA
Money taken from your paycheck before tax for health care. You pick the amount once a year, and it usually has to be spent within the plan year.1
An HSA
Pre-tax money that goes with a high-deductible health plan. It rolls over each year and stays yours if you change jobs.
What makes an expense qualify
Not the service itself, but whether it treats a diagnosed condition. That's why a letter, a clinical note and an itemized invoice matter.2
Who decides
Your plan administrator. Not your therapist, and not us.

What the research says

Massage, and the conditions it has been studied for.

The NIH's research center says massage may help some pain conditions, including low-back pain and chronic neck pain.3

More

Reviews have also found less pain, anxiety and depression for people with fibromyalgia when massage continues over several weeks.

Massage seems to carry few risks when a trained practitioner does it. Some health conditions call for care, so talk with your own clinician.

Evidence is not a promise. It's a reason to ask a clinician whether massage belongs in your care. That's what the consultation is for.

Knowing what to ask for

Common techniques, and what they're for.

Most sessions mix several. Knowing the words helps you say what you need.

Swedish

Long, gliding strokes over the whole body at moderate pressure.

Often the base of a session. On its own, usually for relaxation, not treatment.

Deep tissue

Slower, firmer work into the deeper layers of muscle.

Persistent tightness, chronic back, neck and shoulder pain.

Myofascial release

Sustained, gentle pressure on the fascia, the sheet wrapping muscle.

Restricted movement, pain that spreads beyond one spot.

Trigger point

Focused pressure on tight knots that send pain elsewhere.

Headaches, jaw pain, a shoulder that aches down the arm.

Sports and orthopedic

Work built around a specific movement or injury.

Recovery, overuse injuries, returning to activity.

Prenatal and postpartum

Positions and pressure adapted for pregnancy and after birth.

Back and hip pain, pelvic changes.

Lymphatic drainage

Very light, rhythmic strokes that move fluid.

Swelling and post-surgery recovery, when a clinician recommends it.

A session booked only to relax is not a medical expense, whatever the technique. A diagnosed condition, a treatment for it and a record of both make it care.

For practitioners

Documentation, in short.

  • Write what you saw and did, not a diagnosis. The condition comes from the clinician's letter.
  • Name the areas you worked on, and say if they match the letter. If a session drifts, say why.
  • Record change over time: what your client reported, what you found, what's next.
  • Keep the invoice clean: the treatment on its own, apart from tips, add-ons and retail.
  • Check your state board for licensing, scope and record rules. They differ by state.
